These examples are references for understanding what makes a Minecraft video compelling. They are NOT instructions to copy the creators or reproduce their exact videos.

## Video 1

**Creator:** Wifies
**Style:** Minecraft mystery / investigation / documentary

**Why I like it:**

* Feels like there is an actual mystery to solve.
* The video has a reason to exist beyond "I played Minecraft."
* Information is revealed progressively.
* The viewer feels like they are discovering something alongside the creator.

**Why I would click:**

* The premise creates an immediate question.
* I want to know what the mystery actually is.
* The title/thumbnail can communicate the mystery without explaining everything.

**Why I keep watching:**

* There are unanswered questions.
* New discoveries change what I think is happening.
* The video keeps giving me reasons to continue.

**What makes it different:**

* It treats Minecraft like the setting for a story rather than simply a game.
* The creator is investigating something rather than just completing a generic challenge.

**What I would learn from it:**

* Build the video around a question.
* Don't reveal the entire story immediately.
* Make discoveries progressively more important.

# Patterns I Want The AI To Learn

Strong Minecraft videos in this reference library commonly have:

1. A clear premise.
2. A reason for the viewer to care.
3. A question the video answers.
4. A concrete objective or destination.
5. Progression.
6. Escalation.
7. Setbacks or uncertainty.
8. A meaningful payoff.
9. Strong visual moments.
10. Minimal unnecessary gameplay.
11. A reason for the viewer to continue watching.
12. A distinct personality or storytelling voice.

The goal is NOT to copy these creators.

The goal is to understand the underlying principles behind videos that I personally find compelling.
